# Achieving Sustainable Operations

## Closing the carbon loop

To achieve net-zero carbon emissions around 2045, PUB has embarked on our 3R Decarbonisation Strategy:

![img-83.jpeg](img-83.jpeg)

## REDUCE

carbon emissions by enhancing energy efficiencies

- Seawater desalination is an energy-intensive process. PUB is piloting the use of low-energy technologies to reduce energy use and carbon emissions from this process.

## REPLACE

fossil-fuel based energy with renewable sources

- Large-scale floating solar photovoltaic (PV) systems will enable us to replace carbon-based energy with solar energy for our operations. To date, we have cumulatively deployed 64 megawatt peak (MWp) of floating solar systems, such as the 60 MWp solar farm at Tengeh Reservoir.
- PUB will be calling the tender for the 44 MWp deployment at Pandan Reservoir in the later part of 2023. Concurrently, an environmental impact assessment for the 100 MWp deployment at Lower Seletar Reservoir commenced in 2023.

## REMOVE

carbon through carbon capture and utilisation solutions (CCUS)

- The PUB Carbon Zero Grand Challenge was launched to crowdsource innovative CCUS that can be integrated with PUB's operations. Two awarded projects for proof-of-concept were completed in August 2023. One project involved using a revolving algal biofilm growth system to capture carbon dioxide while simultaneously treating anaerobic digester effluent. The other project involved the capture of carbon dioxide from biogas via a combined solvent absorption and membrane separation process. A catalytic carbon mineralisation process then converted the captured carbon dioxide to solid carbonates.
- PUB is currently piloting the use of an ocean CCUS based on electrochemical technology. It will be able to capture carbon dioxide in seawater as part of the desalination process, while producing hydrogen and softening seawater.

## Closing the waste loop

With limited landfill options, PUB is working towards zero operational waste. To do that, PUB is looking for solutions to reduce and re-use our waste products. For example, under the Competitive Funding for Water Research (CWR) programme, PUB is funding three proposals from Nanyang Technological University (NTU), the Agency for Science, Technology and Research (A*STAR) and Suntar Investment Holding. The funded projects will study the recovery of lithium, calcium and magnesium carbonates from seawater reverse osmosis brine, and the re-use of sludge from used water treatment to produce slag which can be used for non-structural construction like road works.

PUB is also working towards harnessing the water-energy-waste nexus at Tuas Nexus. The co-digestion of treated food waste from NEA's IWMF and used water sludge from PUB's Tuas WRP could produce up to 40% more biogas. This biogas is then channelled back to IWMF and combusted in superheaters to enhance the overall plant thermal efficiency and power generation. Through the co-location of these facilities, we can leverage the synergies between water, energy and waste processes to turn waste into energy.

## Expanding Green Infrastructure through Green Financing

As PUB advances our sustainability agenda, we have established a PUB Green Financing Framework as a guide for raising green financing to meet capital needs for identified green projects.

We issued $800 million in 30-year fixed rate green bonds in August 2022 to support investment in green infrastructure.
