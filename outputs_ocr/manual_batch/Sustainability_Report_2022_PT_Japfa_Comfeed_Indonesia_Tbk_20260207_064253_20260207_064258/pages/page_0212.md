We also monitor the $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ levels through sensors in all poultry divisions, starting from GP to final stock. This is essential to do because if the $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ quantity exceeds the threshold, it indicates that the chickens are not comfortable. In uncomfortable conditions, their respiration rates will increase, which in turn impacts moisture in air and temperature. The $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$ sensors are part of our current control system.

As of 2022, 97% of company-owned farms and close to 80% of farmer partners have implemented this system.

# Ideal Stocking Density

![img-306.jpeg](img-306.jpeg)

Stocking density matters because it impacts the health and well-being of animals. When they are kept in too high of a density, they can become stressed, leading to health problems. High stocking density can also lead to poor air circulation and water quality and an increased risk of disease spread.

The stocking density of our farms and ponds is ideal because it allows us to maximise the farm and pond area and increase productivity while meeting the widely accepted standard for animal welfare practices
